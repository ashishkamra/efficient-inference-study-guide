Formulas

Write the expression first, then say which term a technique moves. A formula with no interpretation is half a mark.

KV footprint

S_kv = 2 · n_layers · n_kv_heads · d_head · s · b · b_bytes

Equation (5). The leading 2 is K versus V. MLA replaces the layout.

k_t = W_UK c_t ,   v_t = W_UV c_t

Equation (11). Store c_t only; reconstruct K and V at attention time.

h_t = Ā_t h_{t−1} + B̄_t x_t ,   y_t = C_t h_t

Equation (10). Input-dependent transitions, fixed-size state.

Operational intensity

OI = FLOPs / bytes moved from main memory

The roofline’s x-axis. Decode at batch 1 is about 1 FLOP/byte in FP16.

Lesson 02

Ridge point

I_ridge = π / β

Lowest intensity that can saturate peak compute π at bandwidth β.

Lesson 02

Roofline bound

P ≤ min(π, β · OI)

Below the ridge, performance tracks bandwidth; above it, the compute ceiling binds.

Lesson 02

Single-stream decode limit

TPOT ≈ 2|θ| / β

Weight bytes divided by bandwidth in FP16. KV traffic and overheads push it higher.

Expected speculative tokens

E[tokens] = (1 − α^(γ+1)) / (1 − α)

Independent acceptance α over a draft of length γ, plus one corrected token.

Speculative speedup

S = E[tokens] / (1 + c · γ)

c is draft cost relative to one target forward. Large batch raises the effective c.

Common traps

Each item states the wrong sentence first, then the correction. Read them as flashcards.

  1. “The 2 FLOPs and 2 bytes are the same factor of two.”

    One 2 is the multiply-accumulate; the other is FP16’s byte width. Changing dtype moves only the byte side, so OI changes.

    Review lesson 02: Bottlenecks, roofline, and the metrics that matter

  2. “KIVI quantizes keys per token and values per channel.”

    It is the reverse: per-channel keys, per-token values. The asymmetry is the whole paper.

    Review lesson 06: Compression and the quality budget

  3. “MLA is just GQA with fewer heads.”

    MLA caches one low-rank latent per token and reconstructs K and V. Equation (5)’s leading 2 no longer applies.

    Review lesson 04: Architectural foundations

  4. “FlashAttention approximates softmax.”

    It is exact attention with an IO-aware schedule. Linear attention changes the math; FlashAttention does not.

    Review lesson 07: Hardware and systems

  5. “Speculative decoding is always lossless.”

    Only the rejection-sampling construction preserves the target distribution. Diffusion, sampled Jacobi, and Skeleton-of-Thought are judged on task quality.

    Review lesson 05: Decoding algorithms

  6. “Paging shrinks the KV cache.”

    Paging fixes allocation and fragmentation. The number of live KV bytes is still equation (5).

    Review lesson 07: Hardware and systems

  7. “Hybrids eliminate the KV cache.”

    They keep a minority of attention layers, so the cache still grows with context on those layers.

    Review lesson 09: Three open frontiers

  8. “A larger batch always improves the user experience.”

    It raises aggregate throughput and can lower cost per token, but it multiplies KV memory and often worsens TPOT.

    Review lesson 02: Bottlenecks, roofline, and the metrics that matter

  9. “Compression savings and quality errors both add linearly.”

    Memory savings usually multiply. Quality errors can compound because later stages calibrate on a model earlier stages changed.

    Review lesson 06: Compression and the quality budget

  10. “Training still dominates the cost of a deployed model.”

    Once a model serves many queries, operational inference dominates TCO. That is the survey’s opening claim.

    Review lesson 01: From training to the inference stack

Oral prompts

Say the answer out loud before opening the sketch. The sketch is a skeleton, not a script.

  1. 01Derive the KV-cache footprint and say which term each pillar moves.

    Start from stored K and V per layer, head, token, and batch. Then: GQA/MLA move n_kv_heads or the layout; quantization moves b_bytes; eviction moves effective s; batching moves b; paging changes allocation, not the count.

    Lesson 03: Autoregression and the KV cache
  2. 02Why is prefill compute-bound while batch-1 decode is bandwidth-bound?

    Prefill has the whole prompt and large parallel matmuls, so arithmetic intensity is high. Decode moves all weights plus the KV cache to emit one token, so bytes dominate. At batch 1 in FP16 the two factors of two cancel to OI ≈ 1.

    Lesson 02: Bottlenecks, roofline, and the metrics that matter
  3. 03Compare MQA, GQA, and MLA on what they store and what they cost.

    MQA stores one KV head, cheap but lossy. GQA stores a group per query-head group and is the production default. MLA stores a latent per token and reconstructs K and V; smaller cache, more compute and a different kernel.

    Lesson 04: Architectural foundations
  4. 04When is speculative decoding lossless, and when does it stop paying?

    Lossless when the verifier rejects and resamples so the marginal distribution matches the target. It pays when decode is bandwidth-bound and τ is high; at large batch verification is no longer nearly free, and drift lowers τ.

    Lesson 05: Decoding algorithms
  5. 05Explain KIVI and why the key/value asymmetry exists.

    Keys are quantized per channel because a few channels carry outliers across tokens; values are quantized per token because their distribution varies token by token. About 2 bits, tuning-free.

    Lesson 06: Compression and the quality budget
  6. 06What does PagedAttention fix, and what does it not fix?

    It replaces max-length contiguous reservations with fixed pages and a block table, removing internal fragmentation and enabling sharing. It does not reduce the live KV footprint or improve the attention math.

    Lesson 07: Hardware and systems
  7. 07Why disaggregate prefill and decode, and what does it change about quantization?

    A long prefill stalls in-flight decodes and inflates TPOT. Separate pools size each phase for its bottleneck. In principle each pool could use a different numeric format; the survey says production examples of that are still missing.

    Lesson 08: The cache and the phase split, full stack
  8. 08State the survey’s three open problems and the tradeoff behind each.

    Recall versus constant state; lossless versus lossy parallel decoding; predictable composition of compression errors. Each is a frontier because the naive combination loses something the survey cannot yet bound.

    Lesson 09: Three open frontiers
